Lawsuit before the alcalde of Valladolid, 1782, in which Juan de Mendoza, a muleteer, sues Pedro Salazar, a merchant, for 340 pesos owed on eleven mules delivered at Medina del Campo in March 1781. The file contains the petition, which states that Salazar paid 100 pesos on delivery and refused the rest; Salazar's answer, which alleges that three of the mules were lame (described as 'cojas e inutiles'); the testimony of four witnesses, named as Diego Perez, a farrier, Ana Ruiz, an innkeeper, and two drovers; and an inspection of the animals dated 4 June 1782. A valuation names 90 pesos as the worth of the three mules. The alcalde's sentence of 19 August 1782 orders Salazar to pay 250 pesos within thirty days and divides the costs between the parties. Salazar's appeal, filed 2 September 1782, is recorded with a receipt for the fee. The file ends with a [ilegible] note in a later hand and the notary's signature, Francisco Blanco. It runs to forty-one folios, in Spanish.
