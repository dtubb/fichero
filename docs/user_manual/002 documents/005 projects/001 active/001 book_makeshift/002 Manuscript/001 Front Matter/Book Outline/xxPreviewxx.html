How do you work when the words come fast, but not well? On a beach, in Puerto Rico, in 2016, watching the waves, I realized I wanted to be a writer: That is, I wanted to work them, to grow them, to prepare them, and mold them, and make words my matter.[^outline_preface_1.] The work to write this book began that morning. It is a book about writing and a book about Colombia, before and after the dawn of AI. Its argument is in favour of a makeshift, improvised, good enough way of working with words, which is much like the way most of the people around the world live and have lived. Writing is hard, not just because thinking is hard, but because it is work. The book is about a makeshift practice, which not unlike ethnography, and that offers an antidote to the head-spinning velocity of generative text and the veneer of rationality and certainty. [^outline_preface_1.]: I borrow Ursula K. Le Guin's turn of phrase here, from the poem at the beginning of her book of essays {guin2019words}.
