### About this Manual

Fichero is a deep app and it has a lot of different features to accommodate different ways of working with archives and research materials, handwritten documents, academic publications and books, historical books, audio recordings, video, email, and plain text files. While it strives to make all of this power as simple and accessible as possible, once you start to use the application, you will see just how much it can do. To help organize all of these tools and techniques, the manual is split into seven parts, along with an appendix. In each part, the features most useful to you during a particular phase of a real-world project are explained in depth.

1. Introduction (Part I): the first section goes over basic installation and updating of Fichero, and explains the key elements of Fichero's interface. This is useful reading for anyone new to Fichero, and a guide to the user interface as you learn the app.

2. Preparation (Part II): next, we'll take a moment to understand the core organizational features of Fichero. Fichero is about keeping track of your archives and files, and allows you to navigate and view different images. You might not need to look at all of the different ways to use the app, but in this section you'll explore how to use it. There may be material here you don't need to read. But if you have questions about how the application is meant to work, this is the section to look at.

3. Transcribing and Workflows (Part III): beyond the obvious aspects of using an app to organize archival and research materials, in this part we go into the many ways you might want to use AI models to transcribe documents, extract useful metadata, construct knowledge graphs, export your materials, and create your own custom workflows.

4. Reading (Part IV): this part is about working with individual documents (reading a transcription beside its source image, flipping between the renditions of a page, drawing regions, and making highlights and annotations). It covers how to read and annotate a document, and how the artifacts you or the AI produce stay anchored to the page they came from.

5. Entities and Knowledge Graph (Part V): this part explains how Fichero — automatically or with AI models — extracts people, places, dates, and other entities out of your documents and turns them into simple statements, each tied to the exact words on the page it came from. It covers how that knowledge graph is built, how to browse it as biographies, maps, and timelines, and how every claim connects back to its source.

6. Search (Part VI): this part covers finding things across your materials — keyword search and semantic search powered by a vector database, scoped to a whole library, a folder, or a set of images, with the matching text shown and the matched words highlighted in the results.

7. Editing (Part VII): getting the most out of Fichero also means correcting what it produces. This part shows how to edit the data (fix transcriptions, merge different spellings of the same person, correct misinterpretations, and edit or delete metadata) so the record improves as you work with it.

8. Appendixes (Part VIII): this part is reference. It documents the menu commands, settings, and views, alongside every tool and workflow, and offers a glossary and a topical index — the miscellaneous material you reach for occasionally, so you can look up an advanced or in-depth topic starting from what you have already found in the interface.
