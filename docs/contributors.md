# Contributors

Fichero is 100% AI coded, under the creative direction of its contributors. Right now, that's just me, [Daniel Tubb](https://tubb.ca).

## Daniel Tubb

<p align="center"><img src="../assets/tubb.png" alt="Daniel Tubb" width="200"></p>

I am an anthropologist and ethnographer working in Colombia.

Since 2016, I have worked in the Department of Anthropology at the University of New Brunswick in Fredericton, Canada. I am the author of *[Shifting Livelihoods: Gold Mining and Subsistence in the Chocó, Colombia](https://uwapress.uw.edu/book/9780295747538/shifting-livelihoods/)*, about the impact of gold mining on communities in Colombia's Chocó department. In addition, I co-edited *[Letters from the Future: How New Brunswickers Confronted Climate Change and Redefined Progress](http://chapelstreeteditions.com/letters-from-the-future.html)*, a book of speculative non-fiction that offers a unique New Brunswick perspective on addressing climate change. Currently, my research focuses on agrarian change and the social lives of lowland crops in the Colombian Pacific.

From that, I have been drawn into working with archives. For that, I wanted a better way to read and work with archives. Fichero is the result of a three-year effort, which began in the summer of 2023, and has been part of ongoing conversations with [Ann Farnsworth-Alvear](https://anthropology.sas.upenn.edu/people/ann-farnsworth-alvear) (History, UPenn), [Andrew Janco](https://pricelab.sas.upenn.edu/fellows/janco-andy), and others in the Chocó, Colombia.

I am no computer engineer, and the last time I took a formal course was in high school, when I wrote a windowing system for a drawing application in Turbo Pascal. However, I have long used snippets of code in my work. As an undergraduate, I taught myself Spanish conjugation by writing an app in REALbasic to quiz me on conjugations. By having to code a Bescherelle, I learned how to conjugate.

As well as a writer and anthropologist, I have been a long-time Mac user. I think of computers as wonderful tools for doing work. But I am also enamoured of what my colleague Noah Pleshet calls [undigital approaches](https://undigital.ca). I believe the method is the message: how we do our work, our research, and our writing is crucial to the work we ultimately produce.

I love AI for fixing my typos, but like many I have become alarmed by the way it is used in the classroom to prevent critical thinking. Yet I am also convinced that AI tools can allow us to do better research — research that would otherwise have been impossible.

Fichero is a case in point. It is the tool I want for reading old archives and historical materials as I write a new book, drawing on a decade and a half of field notes — about a million words — and as I work on a large synthesis project on the origins of agricultural systems in Colombia.

Fichero is at once a tool to help me do this work and a tool for others. It is a Mac shoebox app, inspired by and in many ways complementary to powerful apps — some of which now also have AI features — like [Yojimbo](https://www.barebones.com/products/yojimbo/), [DEVONthink](https://www.devontechnologies.com/apps/devonthink), and [EagleFiler](https://c-command.com/eaglefiler/), which I use alongside citation-management software like [Bookends](https://www.sonnysoftware.com), [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org), and [EndNote](https://endnote.com), and tools to think with and write notes in, like [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) and [Tinderbox](https://www.eastgate.com/Tinderbox/). It also draws inspiration from photo editors like [GraphicConverter](https://www.lemkesoft.de/en/products/graphicconverter/), [Photos](https://www.apple.com/macos/photos/), and [Lightroom](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop-lightroom.html).

What makes Fichero different is that it is designed to make working with many kinds of documents easier — large archival collections, but also interviews, audio recordings, and more. It is fast and easy to navigate. It has a document preview with tools that make archival work easier: a magnification bar at the bottom, and a loupe. It is built on top of powerful open-source software, which gives it semantic search, the ability to create AI workflows that do useful things, and a way to keep track of what has been done.
